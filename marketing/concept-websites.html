Why not just install WordPress for each customer

It was the obvious answer and it was rejected deliberately. A WordPress per owner is far more functionality than the purpose needs, a permanent security exposure across dozens of instances, and an update treadmill that costs more per month than we charge. Its flexibility is exactly what makes it expensive to operate. Our kit is the opposite trade — the owner cannot introduce code of any kind, so the restriction that makes the price possible is the same one that makes it safe (page 5).

Websites — how it sits next to the platform

The website owns its content. Full stop.

The tempting design reads the website live out of the business's listing on the travel platform — one source of truth, no duplication. We deliberately did not do that, and the reasoning is worth reading, because it is the decision the whole product line rests on.

Three reasons it is built this way

Customers with no listing A workshop or a shop will never have one. If the website read from a listing it would have no source for them at all, and two separate data paths would be double the build and double the testing for one product.
The ownership promise We tell a customer the website and its content are theirs. Content read live out of somebody else's record — and disappearing when that record does — is not theirs, whatever the contract says. The architecture has to make the promise true.
Rights on every field The travel platform tracks where each description and each photograph came from. Only material we may actually hand over is imported: third-party directory content is never copied, and material we hold for our own prospecting is marked and blocks publication until it is replaced.

Which is also why it is not only for lodges

The kit is sold just as happily to a restaurant, a workshop or a shop with nothing tourism-bookable about it. A customer with no listing on the travel platform is an equally ranked main case, and gets the same kit starting from empty blocks rather than a cut-down version.

Websites — domains, safety and the legal foot

Hosting other people's businesses is a responsibility, not a hosting bill

Dozens of small sites on one server, each one somebody's livelihood, each one carrying content we did not write. Two things follow: the owner must not be able to introduce code, and the part of the system that can take the server down must not be reachable from the part that serves web requests.

What an owner cannot do Running

No free HTML block, no script, no iframe, no third-party embed, no file upload other than an image that is checked. Formatted text is filtered against a list of permitted markup on the server. A paste out of a word processor cannot put anything executable on their own site.

That restriction is not a limitation we apologise for. It is the reason one person can safely operate a hundred of these.

The legal foot Built

Privacy, legal notice and copyright line on every site. We write the first version and the business owns and edits all three, then confirms them when the site is published.

The system does not generate legal advice. What it generates is a factual description of how this website works — what the form collects, where it goes, that there is no tracking — which we can state because we built it. Everything about the business itself comes off their own record, so a missing address produces a shorter page rather than an invented one.

Where the liability line is drawn

The customer answers for the content; we answer for the hosting and the technology. The technical side already supports that line rather than merely asserting it: because the owner cannot introduce code, everything they publish is exactly what they typed into structured fields — and who published what, and when, is recorded. There is a route for complaints and a way to take a site or a single element offline at short notice.

Websites — selling from the customer's own site

The part no template shop can offer

A lodge's own website normally shows a contact form and a promise to reply. If the business runs on our booking system, its own site can show what is genuinely free tonight and what it costs — read live from the same calendar the front desk works in, never a copy.

What a site can do with the booking system behind it

Live availability and prices Built Read directly from the property's own calendar and rate plan, with the season, the occupancy rules and the taxes included. Never cached, because a cached price is a booking somebody has to honour at the wrong number.
The enquiry form Built Creates a real request in the same pipeline the travel platform uses — so the owner gets the mail with one-click confirm and decline links, and the visitor gets an answer either way. An accommodation is asked for arrival and departure; a restaurant or an activity for a date and a time.
Finishing the booking Built The site quotes, and the guest completes the booking on namibway.com. That hand-over is what exists today and it is what we say, not more.
Booking without leaving Designed Decided and not built: the guest stays on the business's own site throughout, with a sign-in that works across two different domains. That is a real piece of engineering, not a button, and it is named here rather than implied.
Five languages Designed Decided — English, German, Dutch, French and Spanish, matching the travel platform. Today the sites carry the database structure for a second language and nothing else: no switcher, no per-language address. The foundation, not the feature.
The subscription Designed The states below are decided; collection is manual invoicing until a recurring payment method exists in this market. The owner's own create-a-website button stays locked until something knows whether a customer is paying.

Why a travel platform is selling websites at all

Because a lodge's own website is where its direct bookings happen, and a direct booking that runs on our booking system is worth more to both sides than a portal listing. The website line is a distribution channel for the booking system — sold on its own merits to businesses that will never book anything, and sold as one continuous offer to those that will.
